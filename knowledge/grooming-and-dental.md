# Grooming, coat, and dental care

## Brushing by coat type

Short smooth coats (beagles, boxers, most domestic shorthair cats) need a rubber
curry brush once a week. Double coats (huskies, shepherds, corgis) need an
undercoat rake two to three times weekly, and daily during the two heavy sheds
per year. Long silky or curly coats (poodles, doodles, shih tzus, maine coons)
mat within days and need 10-15 minutes of line brushing daily; a mat left
against the skin traps moisture and causes sores.

Brushing is also a health check: run hands over the body for lumps, ticks, hot
spots, and sore points while you work.

## Bathing frequency

Most dogs need a bath every 4-8 weeks. Bathing more often than every two weeks
with a harsh shampoo strips coat oils and worsens itching. Use a pet-specific
shampoo — human shampoo has the wrong pH. Cats groom themselves and rarely need
bathing unless they are elderly, obese, long-haired and matting, or have gotten
into something unsafe to lick off.

## Nails, ears, and paws

Trim nails every 3-4 weeks; if you hear clicking on hard floors, they are too
long. Take small slices to avoid the quick, which recedes gradually as you trim
regularly. Check ears weekly — floppy-eared and swimming dogs are prone to
yeast. A vet-approved ear cleaner and a cotton pad on the outer canal is enough;
never insert a cotton bud. Check between paw pads for grass seeds and packed ice
after every outdoor session.

## Dental care

Periodontal disease affects the majority of dogs and cats by age three and is a
leading source of chronic pain and expense. Daily toothbrushing with an
enzymatic pet toothpaste is the single most effective home measure; three times
a week is the realistic minimum to slow plaque. Never use human toothpaste —
fluoride and xylitol are toxic.

Build the habit gradually over two weeks: let the pet lick the paste, then touch
the brush to the front teeth, then work back. Two minutes a day is enough.
Dental chews and water additives help but do not replace brushing. Bad breath,
drooling, dropping food, or pawing at the mouth warrant a vet dental exam.

## Shedding season logistics

Two heavy sheds per year, usually spring and autumn, roughly double grooming
time for double-coated breeds for about three weeks. Plan for a 20-minute daily
brushing block during those weeks rather than trying to squeeze it into the
usual schedule.
