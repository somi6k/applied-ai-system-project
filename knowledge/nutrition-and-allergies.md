# Nutrition, hydration, and food allergies

## Meal timing and frequency

Adult dogs do well on two meals a day, roughly 10-12 hours apart. Puppies under
six months need three to four meals. Cats are grazers by nature and do better
with three to five small meals or a portioned automatic feeder than one large
bowl. Deep-chested dogs (great danes, standard poodles, weimaraners) should rest
for an hour after eating rather than exercising, which is associated with lower
bloat risk.

Feed at consistent times: a predictable feeding schedule stabilises bathroom
timing, which in turn makes walk scheduling predictable.

## Food allergies and elimination diets

True food allergies most often present as year-round itchy skin, recurring ear
infections, or chronic soft stool rather than as a dramatic reaction. The most
common triggers in dogs are beef, dairy, chicken, and wheat; in cats, beef,
fish, and chicken. Grain-free is not the same as allergen-free — most reactions
are to a protein, not a grain.

Diagnosis requires a strict 8-12 week elimination diet using a novel or
hydrolysed protein, with zero other foods, treats, flavoured medications, or
table scraps. A single lapse restarts the clock. Reintroduce one ingredient at a
time afterwards to confirm the trigger.

## Treats and portion budgeting

Treats should stay under 10% of daily calories; the remaining 90% comes from a
complete and balanced diet. Training-heavy days need the meal portion reduced to
compensate. Use the pet's regular kibble as training rewards when weight is a
concern. Never leave the food out to be topped up ad hoc — measure with a cup or
kitchen scale so intake is actually known.

## Hydration

Cats on dry food only are chronically under-hydrated, which contributes to
urinary tract disease. Wet food, a pet fountain, and multiple water stations
away from the food bowl all increase intake. Dogs need roughly 50-60ml of water
per kilogram of body weight per day, more in heat or after exercise. Refresh
water at least once daily and wash bowls — biofilm puts many pets off drinking.

## Foods to avoid entirely

Chocolate, xylitol (in sugar-free gum and some peanut butters), grapes and
raisins, onions, garlic, macadamia nuts, alcohol, and cooked bones are toxic or
dangerous to dogs and cats. Lilies are fatal to cats even in small amounts.
Keep a 24-hour emergency vet number visible in the kitchen.
