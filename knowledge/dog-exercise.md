# Dog exercise and activity

## Daily exercise needs by energy level

Most adult dogs need 30 to 120 minutes of activity per day, split across two or
more sessions. Low-energy and brachycephalic breeds (pugs, bulldogs, shih tzus)
do well with two 15-20 minute walks. Medium-energy breeds (beagles, corgis,
spaniels) need roughly 60 minutes. High-energy working and sporting breeds
(border collies, huskies, retrievers, shepherds) often need 90 minutes or more,
and a large share of that should be off-leash running or structured work rather
than leash walking.

Split sessions beat one long block: two 30-minute walks tire a dog more evenly
than a single 60-minute walk and give a second bathroom and sniffing break.

## Puppies and growth-plate safety

A common rule of thumb is 5 minutes of formal exercise per month of age, twice
a day — a 4-month-old puppy gets about 20 minutes twice daily. Growth plates
stay open until roughly 12-18 months in large breeds, so avoid repetitive
high-impact activity (long jogs on pavement, jumping off furniture, stair
sprints) until they close. Free play on soft ground where the puppy sets its own
pace is safer than owner-driven distance work.

## Sniffing and decompression walks

Fifteen minutes of sniffing is roughly as mentally tiring as an hour of brisk
walking. A "decompression walk" on a long line in a quiet area, where the dog
chooses the route and pace, lowers arousal and helps reactive or anxious dogs
more than a fast heel-position walk. Schedule at least one loose, sniff-led walk
per day, ideally in the late afternoon when many dogs peak in restlessness.

## Heat, cold, and pavement safety

Walk before 9am or after 6pm when the temperature is above 25C/77F. Test
pavement with the back of your hand for seven seconds — if it is uncomfortable
for you, it will burn paw pads. In cold weather, short-coated and small dogs
need a coat below about 5C/40F, and paws should be rinsed after walks on salted
sidewalks. On days too hot or icy to walk, substitute indoor enrichment: scatter
feeding, tug, stair recalls, or a 10-minute training session.

## Signs a dog is over-exercised

Lagging behind on the return leg, lying down mid-walk, excessive panting that
does not settle within 10 minutes of rest, limping, or stiffness the next
morning all point to too much. Cut duration by a third and rebuild slowly.
Sudden exercise intolerance in a previously fit dog is a reason to see a vet.
