# Cat enrichment and play

## Play sessions that match the hunting cycle

Cats hunt in short bursts, so two to three play sessions of 10-15 minutes beat
one long session. Follow the natural sequence: stalk, chase, pounce, kill, eat.
End every session by letting the cat catch the toy, then feed a meal or a few
treats. Stopping while the toy is still "alive" leaves the cat frustrated and is
a common cause of evening zoomies and night-time waking.

Wand toys that mimic prey movement (dragging away, hiding behind furniture,
short erratic bursts) hold interest far better than a toy waved in the cat's
face. Rotate three or four toys weekly so novelty is preserved.

## Vertical space and territory

Cats measure territory in vertical as well as floor space. A cat tree, a cleared
shelf, or a window perch near a bird feeder gives a resting spot with a view and
reduces conflict in multi-cat homes. The rule of thumb for shared resources is
one per cat plus one: litter boxes, water bowls, beds, and scratching posts.

## Food puzzles and foraging

Feeding some or all of a cat's daily ration from puzzle feeders, snuffle mats,
or treat balls slows eating, adds mental work, and helps weight control. Start
easy — a muffin tin with kibble in a few cups — and increase difficulty once the
cat empties it in under two minutes. Indoor cats who forage for meals show less
over-grooming and attention-seeking behaviour.

## Scratching

Scratching marks territory and sheds nail sheaths; it cannot be trained away,
only redirected. Provide both vertical posts tall enough for a full stretch
(at least 80cm for an adult cat) and horizontal cardboard scratchers, placed
where the cat already scratches and near sleeping spots, since cats scratch on
waking. Trim front nails every 2-3 weeks to limit furniture damage.

## Signs of boredom or stress

Over-grooming to the point of bald patches, night vocalising, ambush attacks on
ankles, litter box avoidance, and eating too fast are all common signs of an
under-stimulated indoor cat. Add a play session before the owner's bedtime and a
foraging meal in the morning before addressing the behaviour directly.
